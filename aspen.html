Pitkin County • Roaring Fork Valley

Aspen, Colorado

A historic silver-mining town reinvented through skiing, art and culture—where Victorian landmarks, mountain adventure and locally rooted businesses share the same compact valley.

Why Aspen Belongs on the Colorado A–Z

From Silver Boomtown to Mountain Cultural Center

Aspen’s story is much deeper than its modern resort reputation. The upper Roaring Fork Valley is ancestral Ute land. Prospectors arrived in 1879, and the settlement—first called Ute City—was renamed Aspen in 1880. Silver transformed it into one of Colorado’s major boomtowns before the 1893 silver crash began decades of economic decline. Skiing revived the community beginning in the 1930s, and the postwar period brought an influential arts, music and intellectual movement that remains central to Aspen’s identity.

Meet Aspen

Mining History, Mountain Adventure & Creative Culture

Aspen grew from one of America’s great silver districts into a world-known mountain community. Its historic streets preserve Victorian-era architecture while Aspen Mountain rises directly above downtown.

Discover the places, people and independent businesses behind the destination.

Local History

Ute Homeland, Silver Boom, Quiet Years & Reinvention

The Ute people lived throughout this region long before Aspen existed. Mining pressure and federal policy culminated in the forced removal of most Ute people from the area in 1881. Silver discoveries brought rapid development: the Denver & Rio Grande Railroad reached Aspen in 1887, the Colorado Midland followed in 1888, and by the early 1890s Aspen was among the country’s leading silver-producing districts.

Repeal of the Sherman Silver Purchase Act in 1893 devastated the local mining economy. During Aspen’s “Quiet Years,” ranching helped sustain the valley. A new era began with skiing in the 1930s. After World War II, Walter and Elizabeth Paepcke helped foster Aspen’s cultural revival and the “Aspen Idea”—a vision linking mind, body and spirit. Skiing, music, ideas, architecture and outdoor recreation have shaped the community ever since.

What Aspen Is Known For

Aspen combines several distinct Colorado stories in one place. It is a former silver boomtown, a major ski destination, a center for music and ideas, a showcase of Victorian and modern architecture, and a gateway to extraordinary mountain landscapes.

Aspen Character

Symbols That Tell the Story

⛏️ Silver Mining

Silver discoveries drove Aspen’s explosive growth in the 1880s and early 1890s, leaving an architectural and industrial legacy still visible today.

🎿 Skiing

Skiing helped revive Aspen after decades of decline, beginning in the 1930s and accelerating after World War II.

🎭 Wheeler Opera House

Opened in 1889, the restored Wheeler remains both a historic landmark and an active venue for music, theater, film, comedy and conversation.

🎨 Arts & Ideas

Aspen’s postwar cultural movement established a lasting identity built around music, art, architecture and intellectual exchange.

🏔️ Mountain Landscape

Aspen Mountain rises directly from downtown, while the surrounding Roaring Fork Valley connects the city to high-alpine recreation and scenery.

🏛️ Historic Preservation

Aspen began formal preservation efforts in the early 1970s and protects significant Victorian and modern-era resources.
