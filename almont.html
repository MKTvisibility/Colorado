Meet Almont

Where Three Rivers Tell the Story

Almont is a small unincorporated Gunnison County community defined by water. Here the Taylor River and East River come together to form the Gunnison River, creating a natural crossroads that has shaped the town from its earliest settlement through today's fishing and rafting culture.

The community was first known as Fishers for early settler Samuel C. Fisher. Almont was platted in 1881 on the Fisher Ranch. Local histories say Fisher proposed the name Almont in honor of the famous Kentucky-bred Hambletonian stallion Almont, sire of Fisher's favored horse. The Almont post office opened in 1882.

Local History

Ranching, Railroads, Mining & Fishing

Samuel Fisher arrived in the area during the late 1870s, established his ranch and helped improve transportation routes through the valley. When railroad service reached the settlement in 1881, Almont became an important transfer point for ore coming down from surrounding mining camps. Ore was brought to Almont and moved by rail toward Gunnison.

Mining eventually declined and the railroad era passed, but Almont's rivers gave the community a lasting identity. Fishing lodges, cabins and ranch resorts developed around its clear mountain waters. Historic accounts describe Almont as a sportsman's destination, with trout fishing becoming an enduring attraction.

Today Almont sits between Gunnison and Crested Butte on Highway 135 and remains a gateway to the Taylor River corridor, Gunnison National Forest and surrounding mountain country. Its rivers support fly fishing and whitewater recreation, while nearby trails and backroads draw hikers, cyclists, horseback riders and off-highway explorers.

What Almont Is Known For

Almont is where rivers and mountain routes meet. The confluence of the Taylor and East rivers, the beginning of the Gunnison River, world-class trout water, rafting, historic ranch and railroad roots, riverside cabins and access to the Gunnison-Crested Butte high country define this tiny Colorado community.

A Few Places to Know

The River Confluence

At Almont, the Taylor and East rivers merge and the Gunnison River begins—a defining landscape feature of western Colorado.

Taylor River Corridor

Follow County Road 742 upstream for fishing, rafting, camping and spectacular mountain scenery on the way toward Taylor Park.

Historic Almont

Rustic cabins, lodges and the small business district preserve the feel of a settlement that grew from ranching, mining transportation and river tourism.

Gunnison National Forest

Surrounding public lands provide extensive opportunities for hiking, mountain biking, horseback riding, camping and winter recreation.
