Meet Alma

A Mining Town More Than Two Miles High

Alma grew from Colorado’s gold-rush era in the upper South Park country. Gold was discovered in nearby Buckskin Gulch, and the earlier settlement of Buckskin Joe became the first major townsite in the area. Hard-rock mines later opened across Mounts Bross, Lincoln and Democrat, drawing thousands of people into what became known as the Mosquito Range Mining District.

Smelting operations helped concentrate settlement at the future site of Alma. On December 2, 1873, smelter employees petitioned Park County for incorporation, and the new town held its first official meeting two days later. According to the Town of Alma, the community was named for Alma James, daughter of a local grocery-store owner.

Local History

Gold, Silver, Smelters & the Mosquito Range

Mining shaped Alma’s early economy and landscape. In the early 1870s, roughly 10,000 people lived in the wider Mosquito Range Mining District. The Boston Colorado Smelting Company and Fanny Barrett Smelting Company established operations in the area, while mines on the surrounding peaks produced valuable ore.

The Paris Mine on Mount Bross opened in 1883. Its Paris Mill, built in 1895, processed ore and later used an aerial tramway to move material from the mine to the mill. The mill operated intermittently into the 1930s and survives as one of the region’s most important mining landmarks.

Alma’s historic buildings tell another side of the story. The Alma School became today's Town Hall and community center; the Alma Community Church, or Stone Church, was built largely by volunteer labor in 1936; and surviving nineteenth-century buildings such as the Spring House preserve traces of the town’s earliest settlement period.

What Alma Is Known For

Alma is a tiny town with an enormous high-country setting. At 10,578 feet, the town promotes itself as North America’s highest incorporated town. Its identity combines mining history, the Mosquito Range, nearby fourteeners, the Middle Fork of the South Platte River, historic buildings and year-round mountain recreation.

Alma Character

Symbols That Tell the Story

⛏️

Mining Heritage

Gold and hard-rock mining created the settlement, while historic mine workings and equipment remain visible throughout the surrounding mountains.

🏔️

Mosquito Range

Mounts Democrat, Cameron, Lincoln and Bross rise above the Alma area and make this one of Colorado’s classic high-alpine landscapes.

⚙️

Paris Mill

The 1895 Paris Mill is a striking survivor of the region’s industrial mining era and was central to processing ore from the Paris Mine.

🌊

South Platte Headwaters

Alma sits where Buckskin Creek meets the Middle Fork of the South Platte River in a narrow mountain valley.

🥾

High-Country Recreation

Hiking, fishing, mountain biking, camping, backcountry travel and nearby fourteeners connect modern Alma to its dramatic landscape.

🏘️

Historic Mountain Town

Historic civic buildings, homes and mining structures preserve the character of a community founded during Colorado’s nineteenth-century mining boom.
