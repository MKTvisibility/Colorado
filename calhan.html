Eastern Plains • El Paso County

Calhan, Colorado

Color-banded badlands, railroad stories, ranch life and generations of community tradition meet in this small Eastern Plains town along U.S. 24.

Paint Mines photo: Lindsey Fisher / El Paso County Parks
Discover Calhan

Small town. Remarkable landscape. Plenty of stories.

Calhan is the closest town to Paint Mines Interpretive Park, but the colorful formations are only the beginning. The community grew with the Rock Island railroad, became home to generations of ranching and farming families, and still gathers around traditions such as the El Paso County Fair. Today, visitors can pair a Paint Mines hike with local food, a historic depot, a working ranch experience and a glimpse of Eastern Plains life.

History & Stories

How Calhan became Calhan

Railroad water, prairie homesteads, a name that lost a letter and a fair that became a county tradition all shaped the community visitors see today.

1888 • Railroad Beginnings

A town born beside the tracks

The Chicago, Rock Island & Pacific pushed west toward Colorado Springs in 1888. A shallow local aquifer made this a useful water stop for steam locomotives, helping establish the settlement that became Calhan.

What's in a Name?

Calahan became Calhan

The settlement was named for railroad contractor Michael Calahan. Local histories record that when the post office opened in 1888, an “a” disappeared from the spelling. The shorter Calhan name stayed.

1906 • Railroad Heritage

The depot that stayed

The surviving Rock Island depot was built in 1906 and served the railroad route between Kansas and Colorado Springs. The town acquired the depot for $1 in 1993, and preservation work has kept this piece of Calhan's railroad story visible.

Homestead Story

Slovak families on the plains

Slovak immigrants were among the homesteaders who settled around Calhan in the 1890s. St. Mary's Eastern Orthodox Church remains part of that historic story, even though MKT Colorado is not currently listing it as a visitor destination.

Since 1905

From Calhan Fair to county fair

The community's fair began as the Calhan Fair in 1905 and grew into the El Paso County Fair. For a time, special Rock Island trains carried fairgoers from Colorado Springs to Calhan.

Thousands of Years

Paint Mines before the town

The colorful clays and formations south of Calhan tell a far older story. El Paso County documents evidence of human use going back thousands of years; the area is protected as an archaeological district as well as a county park.

Did you know? Calhan's railroad depot was purchased for just $1 — and the community's signature fair began more than a century ago, before automobiles replaced special fair trains from Colorado Springs.
Featured Experience

A Day in Calhan: Paint Mines + Small-Town Colorado

Make Calhan more than a quick stop. This easy day-trip outline combines the landscape people come to photograph with local food, history and a hands-on Eastern Plains experience.

Stop 1 • Morning

Paint Mines

Start early at the colorful hoodoos and gullies. Bring water and sun protection, stay on designated trails and leave pets at home.

Stop 2 • Refuel

Eat Local

Head into town for coffee and something sweet at Booey's Bakery, or make Rooster's part of a later lunch stop.

Stop 3 • History

Rock Island Depot

See the 1906 depot and imagine the era when trains connected Calhan with communities across the plains and Colorado Springs.

Stop 4 • Experience

Our Red Barn Ranch

With an advance reservation, finish with alpacas, horses, fiber arts or another hands-on ranch experience just south of town.

Natural & Cultural History

Paint Mines Interpretive Park

Approximately 750 acres of colorful clay formations, hoodoos and prairie with about four miles of trails. The park is free, open dawn to dusk and contains evidence of human use stretching back thousands of years.
