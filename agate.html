Eastern Plains • Elbert County

Agate, Colorado

A small Eastern Plains community with railroad and ranching roots, a memorable namesake landmark and a surviving stretch of the old highway that once carried travelers across Colorado.

Discover Agate

A tiny town with a story worth stopping for

Agate began as a railroad-era settlement in the 1870s and was first known as Gebhard. The community became Agate in 1882. Local history connects the name to an “A-gate,” a wooden livestock gate reinforced with an A-shaped brace—a fitting detail for a place shaped by ranching and the railroad.

Agate was once a much busier stop, with motels, grocery markets, a café, bank, dealerships, lumberyard and grain elevator. Today it is a quiet community surrounded by farms and ranches, where traces of its transportation and agricultural history remain part of the landscape.

Two Pieces of Agate History

Roadside Landmark

The Agate “A-Gate”

One of Agate’s most distinctive local landmarks is an A-braced gate near the center of town. The I-70 Corridor Chamber of Commerce connects this style of livestock gate with the story behind Agate’s name and says the gate still stands off the intersection of Main Street and 2nd Avenue.

Location: Near Main Street & 2nd Avenue, Agate, CO 80101

Historic Drive

Old U.S. Highway 40

Agate sits along the historic U.S. 40 corridor, offering road-trippers a glimpse of the route that carried travelers across the Eastern Plains before Interstate 70 became the dominant highway. It is best experienced as part of a slow drive through the community and surrounding plains.

Location: Historic U.S. 40 through Agate, CO 80101

Respect private property and current road signs while exploring.
