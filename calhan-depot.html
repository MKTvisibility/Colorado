Calhan • Railroad History

Calhan Rock Island Railroad Depot

A 1906 depot preserves the railroad story behind this Eastern Plains town.

The railroad did more than pass through Calhan — it helped create the town. The Chicago, Rock Island & Pacific reached the area in 1888, where a shallow aquifer made the location useful as a steam-locomotive water stop.

Location: 704 Fourth St, Calhan, CO 80808
Built: 1906
Historic reference: History Colorado

A railroad timeline

1888: Rock Island service reaches the area and the settlement develops around the railroad water stop.
1905: The Calhan Fair begins. Special Rock Island fair trains later help bring visitors from Colorado Springs.
1906: The depot that survives today is built to serve the Rock Island route between Kansas and Colorado Springs.
1993: Local history records that the Town of Calhan acquired the depot for $1.
Today: The depot and preserved railroad equipment keep the transportation story visible even though the rails that once connected this corridor are gone.

Why stop?

It is an easy companion to a Paint Mines day trip and a reminder that many Eastern Plains towns make more sense when you follow the old railroad line. Calhan, Ramah and Simla all grew in a landscape where trains once connected small communities to Colorado Springs and points east.
