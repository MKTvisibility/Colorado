Draft original summary:
De Beque grew from ranching in the Colorado River valley, in traditional Ute country. In spring 1884, physician W.A.E. De Beque and three companions explored the surrounding hills while seeking a ranch site. By 1890, the area had 31 ranches. Cattle raising supported the community for decades, followed by growing energy production in the 1920s. Horses also shaped local life: surrounding herds were rounded up and sold, a legacy recognized by the town’s 2001 wild-horse sanctuary designation. The founder’s 1889 house preserves another connection to the settlement’s early years.
